Framework v1.0.3 — Phase 3

Released: 31 July 2026  ·  Scope: Hygiene.

Three items that improve traceability and make the "how much of this can I trust" question answerable at a glance:

  • Chaos-stage billing-export question reworded for pass-fail clarity. The middle option ("Exported, but partial or manual") now reads "Exported, but partial or manual (does not yet cover all providers / accounts)" — makes the Chaos crit-min:70 fail signal explicit to the respondent, so a partial-credit answer no longer surprises them when the stage doesn't pass.
  • Source attribution on the report Method section. New meta.sources[] pointing to the FinOps Foundation FinOps Framework, State of FinOps 2026, and the Flexera State of the Cloud series. Rendered as clickable links (open in new tab) directly on the Method paragraph.
  • Confidence indicator chip alongside the "Critical blockers" tile: Reasonable / Moderate / Low confidence driven by the scored-to-total ratio. Mirrors the FinOps Value Case confidence pattern — a customer looking at their maturity screen can see at a glance whether the sample is thin.
Legal review sign-off: Ashutosh Dixit (ashutosh.dixit@ltm.com)

Framework v1.0.2 — Phase 2

Released: 31 July 2026  ·  Scope: Scoring correctness.

Five items that improve the calibration of the maturity call and give respondents a legitimate "we don't know" answer:

  • "Unsure" (don't-know) answer added to six yes/no questions (billing alarms, finance-access, anomaly detection, egress review, spot interruption handling, per-tenant cost). Unsure answers are excluded from the weighted mean instead of scoring 0, and every Unsure lands on a new Blind spots section of the report with a specific narrative — "anomaly-detection state is unknown — spikes are being discovered by whoever notices the bill first" — so the customer can see exactly what's untested.
  • C4 remediation softened: "Work with your cloud platform team to grant finance read-only billing / cost console access" — respondents typically don't own the RBAC config, so the original directive over-scoped their authority.
  • Culture domain relabelled "Culture & engineering behaviour" so its bar on the "Capability by domain" chart is no longer confusable with the Governance & accountability bar.
  • O10 drag-order is now informational, not scored. Ranking cost levers by delivered value in your environment is legitimately different from any fixed universal ideal; scoring against a fixed order penalised customers with atypical estates.
  • C8 gains an exclusive "None of these" option so respondents can affirmatively say no foundational cost artefacts exist — cleaner than leaving the multi-select empty.
Legal review sign-off: Ashutosh Dixit (ashutosh.dixit@ltm.com)

Framework v1.0.1 — Phase 1

Released: 31 July 2026  ·  Scope: Legal defensibility.

Three items that close labelling and attribution risks identified in the initial deep review:

  • FinOps Foundation non-affiliation notice appended to the disclaimer: "Aligned to FinOps Framework references. Not an official FinOps Foundation assessment or certification. 'FinOps' is a trademark of the FinOps Foundation." The tool has always mirrored the FinOps Framework structurally; the attribution now says so plainly.
  • C1 reframed from an MFA / security-posture question into an account-ownership + access-model FinOps question. Previously, a customer with an MFA gap could fail Stage 1 (Chaos) purely on a security answer, and the tool's remediation ("enforce MFA on root accounts") read as an LTM-issued security finding. C1 now asks whether named account owners and least-privilege billing access exist — the foundation FinOps decisions actually attach to — with a bracketed note pointing MFA back to the CISO.
  • Framework version + reviewed date on the report cover — Framework v1.0.1 · reviewed 2026-07-22. Every maturity screen is now traceable back to a specific build.
Legal review sign-off: Ashutosh Dixit (ashutosh.dixit@ltm.com)

Framework v1.0.0 — Initial release

Released: 22 July 2026  ·  Scope: Baseline.

First customer-facing release of the FinOps Maturity Screen — a staged assessment that walks the customer through five stages (Chaos → Informed → Optimised → Automated → Transparent) and returns the highest contiguous stage passed, together with a horizon-based remediation plan.

  • 44 questions across 5 stages and 5 capability domains (governance, visibility, optimisation, automation, culture).
  • 17 critical questions that must clear a per-stage minimum for the stage to pass. A high average cannot compensate for a missing foundational control.
  • Contiguous-stage progression — maturity is the highest stage passed with every preceding stage also passed. No leapfrogging.
  • 3-horizon remediation plan ranked by (critical, weight, gap size) — short-term critical blockers first, then the next stage, then the horizon beyond.
  • Domain rollup as a "Capability by domain" bar chart, so a strong average doesn't hide weakness in a single domain.
Legal review sign-off: Ashutosh Dixit (ashutosh.dixit@ltm.com)
